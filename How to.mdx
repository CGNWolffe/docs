# How to Write a Police Report

Police reports should always be professional, detailed, factual, and written in chronological order.

\<CardGroup cols={2}\>

  \<Card title="Professional" icon="shield-check"\>

    Use proper law enforcement terminology and maintain a professional tone.

  \</Card\>

  \<Card title="Detailed" icon="file-search"\>

    Include all important observations, officer actions, and enforcement details.

  \</Card\>

  \<Card title="Factual" icon="scale-balanced"\>

    Only include facts, evidence, and direct observations.

  \</Card\>

  \<Card title="Chronological" icon="clock"\>

    Write events in the exact order they occurred.

  \</Card\>

\</CardGroup\>

## Report Writing Rules

\<Steps\>

  \<Step title="Use Professional Language"\>

    Avoid slang, jokes, abbreviations, or personal opinions.

  \</Step\>

  \<Step title="Write in Chronological Order"\>

    Start from the first observation and continue until the scene is cleared.

  \</Step\>

  \<Step title="Include Observations"\>

    Clearly explain what you saw, heard, smelled, or observed.

  \</Step\>

  \<Step title="Document Officer Actions"\>

    Include stops, arrests, searches, citations, and evidence collection.

  \</Step\>

  \<Step title="Explain the Outcome"\>

    State how the incident ended and what enforcement action was taken.

  \</Step\>

\</Steps\>

---

## Narrative Structure

A proper narrative should include the following sections:

\<AccordionGroup\>

  \<Accordion title="1. Initial Observation"\>

    Explain why you became involved in the incident.

  \</Accordion\>

  \<Accordion title="2. Vehicle or Suspect Actions"\>

    Describe what the suspect or vehicle was doing.

  \</Accordion\>

  \<Accordion title="3. Officer Response"\>

    Explain what actions were taken by law enforcement.

  \</Accordion\>

  \<Accordion title="4. Investigation"\>

    Document statements, evidence, sobriety tests, searches, and observations.

  \</Accordion\>

  \<Accordion title="5. Enforcement Action"\>

    Include charges, citations, arrests, or warnings issued.

  \</Accordion\>

  \<Accordion title="6. Scene Conclusion"\>

    Explain how the scene ended and final disposition.

  \</Accordion\>

\</AccordionGroup\>

# Example Traffic Stop Report

\`\`\`text

On 05/22/2026 at approximately 2135 hours, I was on patrol in the area of Route 68 when I observed a black Dodge Charger travelling at a high rate of speed.

I activated my emergency lights and siren and conducted a traffic stop near the intersection of Route 68 and Joshua Road. The vehicle came to a stop on the right shoulder.

I approached the driver side of the vehicle and made contact with the driver, later identified as John Smith. I advised the driver of the reason for the stop and requested their driver's licence, registration, and proof of insurance.

While speaking with the driver, I observed signs of impairment, including slurred speech, bloodshot eyes, and the odour of alcohol coming from the vehicle.

The driver was asked to exit the vehicle and perform standardized field sobriety tests. Based on my observations and the results of the investigation, the driver was placed under arrest for impaired operation of a motor vehicle.

The suspect was searched incident to arrest and transported to the station for processing. The vehicle was towed from the scene without incident.

\`\`\`

## Good vs Bad Reports

\<CardGroup cols={2}\>

  \<Card title="Good Report" icon="badge-check"\>

\`\`\`text

On 05/22/2026 at approximately 2010 hours, I observed a white Ford Explorer fail to stop at a posted stop sign near Bay Street and Lakeshore Road.

I initiated a traffic stop using my emergency lights. The vehicle stopped without incident.

I contacted the driver and issued a citation for failure to stop at a stop sign.

\`\`\`

  \</Card\>

  \<Card title="Bad Report" icon="triangle-exclamation"\>

\`\`\`text

Pulled guy over. Ran stop sign. Gave ticket.

\`\`\`

This report lacks:

- Location

- Time

- Officer actions

- Vehicle information

- Enforcement details

  \</Card\>

\</CardGroup\>

## Helpful Report Writing Tips

\<Tip\>

Always include exact locations, road names, postal numbers, or intersections whenever possible.

\</Tip\>

\<Tip\>

Document all emergency equipment usage including lights, sirens, and pursuit involvement.

\</Tip\>

\<Tip\>

Vehicle descriptions should include:

- Make

- Model

- Colour

- Plate Number

\</Tip\>

\<Tip\>

If evidence was collected, document exactly where it was found and how it was secured.

\</Tip\>
