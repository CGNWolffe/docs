The Ontario Provincial Police (OPP) uses both the NATO and APCO phonetic alphabets. Phonetics are used to spell out words, names, addresses, and licence plates clearly over the radio, so that letters which sound alike (B, D, P, T, V and C, S, Z) cannot be confused across a noisy or weak transmission.

Because the OPP draws on both systems, members should be able to send in one and receive in either.

\<Info\>

Only two letters share the same word across both systems: **V (Victor)** and **X (X-ray)**. Every other letter differs, which is why mixed usage is the most common source of confusion.

\</Info\>

## Numbers

Numerals are transmitted digit by digit. Three and nine are given modified pronunciations to keep them distinct.

| Digit | Spoken as | Pronunciation |

| :---: | --------- | ------------- |

| 0 | Zero | ZEE-roh |

| 1 | One | WUN |

| 2 | Two | TOO |

| 3 | Three | TREE |

| 4 | Four | FOW-er |

| 5 | Five | FIFE |

| 6 | Six | SIX |

| 7 | Seven | SEV-en |

| 8 | Eight | AIT |

| 9 | Nine | NIN-er |

## How to use phonetics

\<Steps\>

\<Step title="Pick one system and stay with it"\>

Do not mix NATO and APCO within a single transmission. Choose one and use it for the whole spelling.

\</Step\>

\<Step title="Give the letter, then the word"\>

Say B as in Bravo, rather than Bravo alone. This gives the receiver two chances at it.

\</Step\>

\<Step title="Slow down"\>

Phonetics only work if each word is fully spoken. Rushing defeats the purpose.

\</Step\>

\<Step title="Spell numbers digit by digit"\>

Say four two seven, never four twenty-seven.

\</Step\>

\<Step title="Read back in the sender's system"\>

When repeating back, use the same alphabet the sender used so any discrepancy is obvious.

\</Step\>

\</Steps\>

\<Warning\>

Zero and the letter O are the most commonly confused pair on air. Say figure zero if there is any doubt.

\</Warning\>

## Points to watch

\<AccordionGroup\>

\<Accordion title="Alfa and Juliett are spelled that way on purpose" icon="spell-check"\>

Alfa drops the ph and Juliett carries a double t so that speakers of any language pronounce them correctly. Both are the official spellings.

\</Accordion\>

\<Accordion title="Quebec is pronounced keh-BECK" icon="volume-high"\>

The NATO alphabet specifies this pronunciation, which differs from the anglicized kwuh-BECK.

\</Accordion\>

\<Accordion title="APCO wording varies between agencies" icon="triangle-exclamation"\>

Some services use Oboe for O and Uncle for U. Treat the words on this page as the standard, and confirm rather than assume when a caller or outside agency uses a different word.

\</Accordion\>

\<Accordion title="X-ray is the only non-name entry" icon="x"\>

It appears in both systems and is spoken as two syllables, ECKS-ray.

\</Accordion\>

\</AccordionGroup\>
