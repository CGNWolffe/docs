During a pursuit, dispatch controls the channel. Everything that is not related to the pursuit waits.

## Declaring emergency traffic

<Steps>

<Step title="Acknowledge and take the channel">
When a unit declares a pursuit or emergency traffic, acknowledge immediately and advise all units to hold their transmissions.
</Step>

<Step title="Broadcast the essentials">
Direction of travel, current street, vehicle description, plate, and the reason for the pursuit.
</Step>

<Step title="Get a supervisor on the air">
A supervisor must be aware and monitoring. Note when they acknowledge.
</Step>

<Step title="Log continuously">
Time, location, and speed at each update. This record matters afterward.
</Step>

<Step title="Clear the channel when it ends">
Announce that emergency traffic is over and normal traffic may resume.
</Step>

</Steps>

## What dispatch broadcasts

| Field | Why it matters |
| ----- | -------------- |
| Direction of travel | Lets other units position ahead rather than chase from behind |
| Street and cross street | The only way units can converge accurately |
| Vehicle description and plate | Identifies the vehicle if it is lost from view |
| Speed and driving behaviour | Feeds the decision on whether to continue |
| Reason for the pursuit | Determines whether the risk is justified |

<Warning>
If the primary unit loses visual contact, say so on the air immediately. A pursuit that continues on assumption is more dangerous than one that is called off.
</Warning>

## Support to start automatically

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="Secondary unit" icon="car">
One backup unit joins as secondary. Additional units stay off the pursuit and position ahead instead.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Supervisor" icon="user-tie">
Monitors and holds the authority to terminate.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Air support and canine" icon="helicopter">
Start early if available. Air support often allows ground units to back off entirely.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="EMS on standby" icon="truck-medical">
Place EMS on standby for any pursuit at speed, before anything happens rather than after.
</Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>

<Note>
Pursuit authority and termination criteria are policy decisions. Replace this page's guidance with your organization's actual pursuit policy.
</Note>
